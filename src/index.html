Motion-aware layouts

Built for a body that moves.

A picture that runs from your chest down your arm breaks the moment you reach for something. So every look keeps each zone self-contained.

  • Torso scenes stay framed in the torso. Chest and stomach can join into one screen because they move together.
  • Sleeves get things that survive motion: vertical stripes, tickers, a clock, a level meter, solid color.
  • Cuffs are light bands: a color or a pulse, never a picture.
  • The layout knows the garment. The same look re-flows onto a shoe or a cardigan, zone by zone.

What it plays

Six ways to use a zone.

SHOWS

Your shows, on your stomach

Host whole episodes of anything you have the rights to. People watch while they talk to you.

PHONE

Mirror your phone

Send a playlist, a map or a photo from your phone to one zone, live.

CODE

Write what it draws

A tiny canvas API per zone. A plasma, a ticker, a game. Code is sandboxed and checked for safe flash rates before it plays.

DRAW

Write on your back

Type or draw on your phone and it shows up on the zone you pick. Say what you didn't get to say out loud.

GAMES

Play on your chest

Tic-tac-toe today. Anything turn-based that someone can tap on their own phone tomorrow.

AD SLOTS

Rent space that follows you

Ad slots belong to your account, so they move with you onto every Exovela piece you wear. You choose who rents them.

How we'd make it real

A first sweater from parts on sale today.

Why a sweater first: thick knit hides flexible LED panels and diffuses them into soft pixels, so it looks like a sweater when it's off. A thin shirt would show every board.

The ecosystem

Buy the piece once. Change it every day.

THE BASE PIECE

One garment, any look

You buy the sweater or the shoe. Looks are software, so the same piece is a flannel at lunch and a fireplace at night.

CREATOR MARKET

Design, sell and buy looks

A free marketplace for looks, widgets and games. Creators set their price, and every look is checked for fit, power and flash safety before it's listed.

How creators get paid

MADE TO ORDER

New styles, made on demand

We're working toward a manufacturing partner that can make new cuts in small runs, so a popular design can become a real garment without a big factory order. That partnership is in the works; it doesn't exist yet.
