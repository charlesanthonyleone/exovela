How we’d build it

A first wearable can be built from parts on sale today.

Stage costumes already carry flexible LED panels that play video. Prototype v0 uses the same parts, rearranged so the electronics live in a sealed shell and the battery stays in the washable layer underneath. The catch is resolution: about 96 × 144 pixels across the front. That's sharp from a few metres away and coarse up close.

CONTROLLER

A small computer and open-source driver

A Raspberry Pi Zero 2 W with an LED matrix driver board plays clips that have been pre-scaled to the panels. A phone page picks the channel and sets brightness, within a cap.

POWER

About 3.5 hours, estimated

A 20,000 mAh USB-C power bank in the inner suit, at night brightness. Every clip passes a power limiter and a flash check before it can play. Runtime will be measured, not quoted.

CARE

Splash-proof first, sealed later

v0 aims for splash resistance with coated panels and a gasketed pod. The inner suit holds only a sealed cable once the battery is out, so it goes in the wash. Full sealing comes with custom tiles.

Estimated parts cost for one v0 garment: about $510 before shipping, about half of it confirmed from seller listings in November 2026. Nothing here has been built or tested yet. v1 moves to custom sealed LED tiles shaped to the pattern pieces, with the controller and battery in the spine pack.

System

Garment and platform, end to end.

RISK

Heat and breathability

A sealed display blocks vapor. Tile gaps, vents and fans have to be proven on a body, in warm weather.

RISK

Flex life

Interconnects at the elbows, shoulders and hood hinge are the most likely failure points.

RISK

Public-screen safety

Flash limits, brightness caps for night and driving, and moderation backed by human review.
