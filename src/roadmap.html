Roadmap

The first sweater can be heavy. Then it gets light.

The first two pieces only have to prove the idea works on a body: a thick knit sweater and a sneaker. Everything after that is about making them lighter, better-looking, cheaper and available in more pieces. The phases below are in order. They are plans, not dates.

PHASE 0 · NOW

Concept and disclosure

Write the invention down in full, take it to a registered patent attorney, and commission a professional prior-art search before anything is filed. Status is on the technology page.

PHASE 1 · BENCH RIG

Prove the panel and the seal

Build flat test panels from off-the-shelf flexible LED parts, behind knit and potted in clear silicone. Wash them, flex them, scuff them, and measure power draw, heat and brightness through the fabric directly instead of estimating them.

PHASE 2 · PROTOTYPE 01

Sweater

A thick knit sweater with panels on the chest, stomach and back, strips on the sleeves and bands at the cuffs, all in a removable inner layer. It is allowed to be heavy. The goal is an evening of wear, a working phone app and the first looks.

PHASE 3 · PROTOTYPE 02

Shoe

A knit sneaker with sealed zones on both sides, the heel, toe cap, tongue and sole, a heel pod with a motion sensor, and an inner bootie that washes. It tests how LEDs survive a foot that bends with every step.

PHASE 4 · LIGHTER

Custom tiles

Sealed LED tiles cut to the pattern pieces and made in volume. Lighter, thinner, more of the surface lit, smaller batteries. The sweater has to look like a good sweater with the display off.

PHASE 5 · MORE PIECES

Joggers, cardigan, V-neck

More pieces on the same tiles, controller and app, each with its own layout. A thinner knit needs finer pixels, so the V-neck comes after the cardigan. The T-shirt waits for stretchable displays.

PHASE 6 · WITHIN REACH

An entry line

Fewer, smarter zones and a core you move between pieces, so one battery and controller go from your winter sweater to your spring shoes. Joggers start with simple color zones, since legs bend constantly.

Joggers, colored

One cut, any color.

The first joggers keep one cut and get simple color zones, since legs bend all day. You pick the color, or let it slowly shift. Tap a swatch to try it.

Pricing

Premium first, like every new category.

The first garments will be expensive, because the display shell is the costly part and early production is small. Like the first phones and the first flat-screen TVs, the price comes down as panels get cheaper and volumes grow. At launch the position is simple: this is the thing people have wanted from clothing for decades, and the technology to make it is finally here.

No retail price is set. The cost picture is in the estimated bill of materials, held in the private disclosure draft.
