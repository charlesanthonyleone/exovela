Roadmap

The first hoodie can be thick. Then it gets thin.

The first garment only has to prove that the two-layer system works. Everything after that is about making it lighter, better-looking, cheaper and available in more cuts. The phases below are listed in order. They are plans, not dates.

PHASE 0 · NOW

Concept and disclosure

Write the invention down in full, take it to a registered patent attorney, and commission a professional prior-art search before anything is filed. Status is on the technology page.

PHASE 1 · BENCH RIG

Prove the dock and the seal

Build a flat test panel from off-the-shelf flexible LED matrix tiles laminated in clear polymer, plus a spine pack and a magnetic dock. Test it under water, under spills, flexed and abraded. Measure the power draw and heat directly instead of estimating them.

PHASE 2 · PROTOTYPE 01

Hoodie

A wearable hoodie with display zones on the chest, back and sleeves, mounted on a ventilated inner suit. It is allowed to be thick. The goal is a full day of wear, a working studio app and the first skins.

PHASE 3 · PROTOTYPE 02

Jacket

A structured jacket shell, where the stiffer shape suits larger, flatter panels and higher-resolution display areas. It also tests the perimeter attachment and the charging hanger.

PHASE 4 · LIGHTER

Weight, coverage and looks

Thinner panels, more of the surface lit, smaller batteries, and better venting. The shell has to look like a premium jacket even with the display off.

PHASE 5 · CUTS

Base looks

A range of shells over shared inner suits, including the straight jacket, the suit jacket and future cuts, each with its own silhouette and display layout.

PHASE 6 · PANTS

Stiff pants and joggers

Pants come in a fixed style, and you color them digitally. Legs bend constantly, so pants use simpler, lower-resolution color zones rather than a full display, which keeps them light, durable and easy to charge.

Pants, colored

One cut, any color.

The pant shell keeps one tailored style. You pick its color, or let it slowly shift. Tap a swatch to try it.

Pricing

Premium first, like every new category.

The first garments will be expensive, because the display shell is the costly part and early production is small. Like the first phones and the first flat-screen TVs, the price comes down as panels get cheaper and volumes grow. At launch the position is simple: this is the thing people have wanted from clothing for decades, and the technology to make it is finally here.

No retail price is set. The cost picture is in the estimated bill of materials, held in the private disclosure draft.
