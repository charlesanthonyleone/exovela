How it works

Five layers.
One garment.

Your skin, the inner suit, the dock that joins the two layers, the display, and the clear coat that protects it. Each layer has one job, and none of them makes another layer's job harder.

The two pieces

Inner suit, then the shell.

Two pieces that come apart. Switch the cut, pull the layers open, or clip a section through the dock to see how they meet.

Outer shell — sealed display, never washed Inner suit — knit, thermal channels, washable Dock — magnets and pogo pins, sealed on the shell Spine pack — battery, controller, harness
5 · PROTECTIVE COAT

Clear, hard, slippery

A clear layer like a phone screen protector: a hard-coated polymer film on flexible areas, and optionally ultra-thin glass on flatter panels. It resists scratches and stains, and fibers slide off it instead of catching.

4 · DISPLAY

The canvas

Flexible emissive panels such as flexible OLED or micro-LED on film, or e-paper for low-power designs, tiled to follow the garment's pattern pieces. Everything is fully encapsulated, so the panels never see water.

3 · DOCK

Where the layers meet

The single point where power and data pass between the layers, held by magnets and sealed on the shell side. Around it, a perimeter attachment such as a zip, snaps or magnetic tape holds the two layers together, the way zip-in liners do in a 3-in-1 jacket.

2 · INNER SUIT

The working layer

A breathable, washable knit with spacer-mesh channels that move heat and moisture away from the body. It carries a removable spine pack holding the battery and controller, and it takes the dirt so the shell doesn't have to.

1 · YOU

The body

Only the inner suit touches skin. You put it on first and wash it like normal activewear once the spine pack is out. The shell goes on over it and docks with one click.

THE RULE

Never wash the light

Every design decision follows one rule: the electronics that make light live in a sealed shell that never needs laundering. That was the missing piece in every light-up garment before this.

Systems

How the layers share work.

Power

The battery sits in the inner suit's spine pack, against the back, where weight is easiest to carry. It powers the shell through the dock. Charging can be done by plugging in the pack or by hanging the whole garment on a charging hanger. How long a charge lasts depends heavily on the display type and on how bright and busy the design is, which is why the platform shows a power estimate for every skin.

Heat and air

A display shell is close to airtight, so heat and moisture management is the hardest engineering problem here, and the inner suit is built around it. Spacer channels carry warm, moist air toward vents at the underarms, back yoke and hem, which can be passive or fan-assisted. The shell can also be built as sealed tiles on a breathable carrier, so air moves through the gaps between tiles.

Data

The controller in the spine pack stores skins and plays them. Your phone sends new skins over a short-range wireless link. The shell receives only the pixel data it needs through the dock, so the shell itself stays simple.

Care

The inner suit gets washed once the pack is removed. The shell gets wiped with a soft cloth and stored on its hanger. There are no exposed metal contacts on the shell, and the dock is sealed and recessed, so rain, spills and sweat have nowhere to get in.

Base settings

Adjustable before you ever write code.

Brightness

Automatic or manual, with a night-safe limit for driving and dark rooms.

Motion

Speed, pause, and a still mode that holds a single frame.
